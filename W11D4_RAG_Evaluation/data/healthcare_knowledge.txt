Healthcare Knowledge Base

1. Diabetes

Diabetes is a chronic condition in which blood glucose levels remain higher than normal. Type 1 diabetes occurs when the immune system attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body does not use insulin effectively or does not produce enough insulin. Common symptoms of diabetes can include increased thirst, frequent urination, increased hunger, fatigue, and blurred vision. Diabetes can be managed through healthy eating, physical activity, medication when prescribed, and regular monitoring of blood glucose levels.

2. Hypertension

Hypertension, commonly called high blood pressure, is a condition in which the force of blood against artery walls remains consistently elevated. Blood pressure is measured using systolic and diastolic values. Persistent hypertension can increase the risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke, kidney disease, and other health problems. Lifestyle measures such as reducing sodium intake, maintaining a healthy weight, exercising regularly, avoiding tobacco, and limiting alcohol can help manage blood pressure. Some people may also require prescribed medication.

3. Asthma

Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition that causes inflammation and narrowing of the airways. Symptoms may include wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath. Asthma symptoms can be triggered by allergens, respiratory infections, exercise, smoke, and other environmental factors. Treatment commonly involves inhaled medicines prescribed by a healthcare professional. People with asthma may also have an action plan for managing symptoms and attacks.

4. Anemia

Anemia occurs when the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to body tissues. Iron deficiency is one common cause of anemia. Symptoms may include tiredness, weakness, dizziness, pale skin, and shortness of breath. Treatment depends on the underlying cause and may include dietary changes, iron supplementation when appropriate, or treatment of an underlying medical condition.

5. Common Cold

The common cold is a viral infection that usually affects the upper respiratory tract. Symptoms can include a runny nose, sore throat, coughing, sneezing, and mild fatigue. Most common colds improve with rest, fluids, and supportive care. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections such as the common cold. People should seek medical advice when symptoms are severe, persistent, or concerning.

6. Influenza

Influenza, or flu, is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. Symptoms may include fever, cough, sore throat, muscle aches, headache, chills, and fatigue. Influenza can sometimes lead to complications, particularly in people at higher risk. Annual influenza vaccination is an important preventive measure. Treatment may include supportive care and, for some patients, antiviral medication prescribed by a healthcare professional.

7. Heart Disease

Heart disease refers to several conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels. Coronary artery disease is one common form and occurs when the arteries supplying the heart become narrowed or blocked. Risk factors can include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, physical inactivity, and family history. A heart-healthy lifestyle can include regular physical activity, balanced nutrition, avoiding tobacco, and managing conditions such as hypertension and high cholesterol.

8. Kidney Health

The kidneys filter waste and excess fluid from the blood and help regulate fluid, electrolytes, and blood pressure. Chronic kidney disease involves a gradual loss of kidney function over time. Diabetes and hypertension are important risk factors for chronic kidney disease. Regular medical monitoring can help identify kidney problems, particularly in people with risk factors.

9. Nutrition

A balanced diet provides the body with carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, fiber, and water. Fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and other nutrient-dense foods can contribute to a healthy diet. Individual nutritional needs vary depending on age, activity level, health conditions, and other factors. People with specific medical or dietary needs should seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian.

10. Preventive Healthcare

Preventive healthcare focuses on reducing the risk of disease and detecting health problems early. Preventive measures can include vaccinations, recommended health screenings, regular physical activity, balanced nutrition, adequate sleep, avoiding tobacco, and appropriate medical checkups. Recommended screenings and preventive measures vary according to factors such as age, sex, medical history, and individual risk.
